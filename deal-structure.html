Why Deals Die

Deals die because of poor deal structure. Not because the business was bad.

There is a lot of noise and “training” about the typical 80/10/10 SBA structure. Buyers and searchers follow it, and then the LOI comes back declined, the loan stalls at underwriting, or the lender stops answering after the second or third failed attempt. The business was fine. Their profile was fine. The deal was never bankable as written. Getting the right structure in place and taking it to the right lender is the difference between a quick no and a countersigned LOI, and both are worth having.

01

Price against what a lender will finance

Not what the seller asks or what the buyer can stretch to. The number a credit desk will carry at a coverage ratio it can defend, on each of the last three years, not only the best one.

02

Equity injection and the seller note

How much cash down, how much on a seller note, how long it sits on standby, and whether an earnout or a holdback belongs in the deal at all. Most declines trace back to this box.

03

Debt-service coverage, year by year

Lenders run coverage on every historical year, with working capital and the owner’s replacement salary included. A growing business priced on this year’s earnings fails on last year’s unless the structure accounts for it.

04

The right financing path and the right desk

SBA 7(a), conventional, a combination with real estate carved out, or a seller-financed bridge. Then the specific lender whose credit box fits the deal, so the file lands on a desk that has funded this kind of business before.

Questions Buyers Ask

Before you send the next LOI.

The business was good and my profile was good. Why was my LOI declined or my loan stalled?
In our experience, most of the time because the deal was not bankable as written. The price was set against the seller’s hopes rather than against what a lender will carry, the equity injection or seller note did not fit the lender’s credit policy, the seller note was not on standby, or debt-service coverage failed on one of the historical years the bank looks at. Brokers decline LOIs they do not believe will finance, lenders stall files that fail a test they do not always explain, and after the second or third failed attempt many stop replying. None of that says anything about the business or about you. It says the structure needs to change before the next offer.
Is the 80/10/10 SBA structure the right one for my deal?
Sometimes, and far less often than the training courses suggest. Eighty percent bank debt, ten percent seller note and ten percent buyer equity is a starting template, not a rule, and every lender layers its own credit policy on top: how long the seller note must stand by, what counts toward the injection, how working capital and the owner’s replacement salary enter the coverage calculation, and how much of the price can be goodwill. The right structure depends on the business, the lender and the buyer, and it is usually settled in the LOI, not at the bank.
Is the SBA financing fit check a lender pre-approval?
No. Only a lender can pre-qualify or approve a loan, and we are not a lender. The fit check applies the tests an SBA credit desk will apply: program eligibility, the purchase price against the SOP thresholds for a valuation and a Quality of Earnings report, the equity injection and seller-note structure, and debt-service coverage on each historical year. It tells you whether the deal is likely to finance as presented and what would need to change if not. When a deal fits, we introduce you to a lender in our network who can run a real pre-qualification, usually within a few days.
Can I send any listing for a deal review?
Any listing from any broker, marketplace or direct outreach, with one exception: businesses where Investment Consulting USA represents the seller, because an opinion on a deal where we are paid by the seller is not independent. If a deal you send turns out to be one of them, we tell you before any work starts and your review is not used up.
What is a deal review, and when do I need a Quality of Earnings report instead?
A deal review works from what the seller has already published: the CIM or broker summary, the financial recap and, if you have them, tax returns. It is built for the search stage, when you are deciding which of several listings deserves your attention. A Quality of Earnings report tests the earnings against bank statements, tax returns and source documents once you are under LOI, and SBA lenders must commission one on acquisitions of $3 million or more. QofE reports and appraisals come from Kerdai, the group’s independent QofE and appraisal firm.
