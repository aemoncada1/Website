        What this estimate can’t see

        • Recurring revenue. Service agreements, contracts and repeat customers raise what buyers will pay.
        • How much runs through you. If key customers, licenses or relationships depend on you, buyers discount for that risk.
        • Customer concentration. One customer above 15–20% of sales worries buyers and lenders.
        • Financing. Many buyers borrow to buy, often with an SBA loan. If a lender won’t finance the price, the price usually comes down. We can check this before you go to market.
        • Add-backs. Personal or one-time expenses run through the business can raise your earnings, and your value, when they’re documented.

        Three questions owners ask first

        Will anyone find out my business is for sale?
        No. Nothing here is a listing. Your answers are seen only by the two senior advisors, and if you ever decide to sell, every buyer signs a confidentiality agreement before your company is identified.
        What is in the Broker’s Opinion of Value, and what does it cost?
        It is complimentary. After the call, a senior advisor sends a written opinion of what your business would likely command from qualified buyers today, the earnings a buyer and a lender would count, and the specific factors that would move the number. It is not a certified appraisal; if you need one for legal, tax or lending purposes, we will say so.
        I am not ready to sell. Is this still for me?
        Yes. Most of the owners we work with are one to three years away. Knowing the range now, and what moves it, is how you sell from strength later instead of urgency.
